# Add an Opportunity

This is the **Add an Opportunity** card on the Put It to Work screen. Whenever you have a specific job description in hand, choose Add an Opportunity, paste it in, and Reimagine creates an Opportunity Playbook for that role. Three sections start building the moment you submit the posting; the rest you build on demand. It is the most natural reason to come back to Reimagine during an active search: every specific role you consider can get its own playbook here. You do not have to explore directions first. Add an Opportunity is available as soon as your Personal Brand exists, and it is a permanent sidebar entry under Put It to Work, reachable from anywhere in the app.

## When to use it

Career Paths gives you a strategy: who you are, where you fit, three directions through your possible options, and a full Focus Playbook for any role worth your attention.

That strategy gets you ready to act. Specific roles come into view: a posting your network forwarded you, a job description you found on LinkedIn, an opportunity you uncovered through your outreach. When one of those lands in your sights and you want to be sharp for it, this module builds a playbook for that exact role.

Use it when:

- You are seriously considering applying to a specific posting
- You have a screening call or first conversation scheduled
- You want to know whether a role is the right fit before you invest more time in it
- You want a Bridge Story or interview prep tuned to this specific posting

## What this module produces

You paste a job description or upload it as a PDF. Reimagine creates an Opportunity Playbook scoped to that role. The section rail on the left lists ten entries, in this order: **About This Company · Who You Know Here · Compensation · Where you fit · Resume Refresh · Cover Letter · Interview Team · Interview Prep · Offer & Negotiation · Recruiters for This Role.**

Three of them — About This Company, Compensation, and Where you fit — start building the moment you submit the posting, because they need nothing from you beyond the job description itself. The rest you build when you want them. Every section is saved with this opportunity:

- **Where you fit** — an honest read on alignment with the direction you are pursuing, where the role stretches you, and the watch-outs you should know going in. It finishes with a **30-second bridge story** tuned to this posting: the case above in words you can say out loud. The bridge builds automatically right after the read, and you can refine it in its own feedback box until it sounds like you saying it. (This is the Opportunity Playbook's equivalent of the section a Focus Playbook calls **The Role**. Same job, different name on the screen, because here the read is against one specific posting rather than a whole direction.)
- **Resume Refresh** — a repositioned summary and key accomplishments that emphasize the competencies this role asks for.
- **Cover Letter** — a short draft letter tuned to this posting, in your own voice. It is the sibling of your Go-to-Market outreach: the same direct, human energy, adapted for when you are responding to a posting. It is part of your application packet with your bridge story (built into Where you fit) and Resume Refresh, tuned so they read as one voice rather than repeating each other. It draws on those two, so building them first makes the letter stronger (it will still generate without them). It addresses the company by name, so it needs a posting Reimagine can identify the company from. Drawing on your About This Company research, it opens to the most relevant leader by first name when the research names one whose area covers the role, and to the team by function otherwise (no "Dear Hiring Manager").
- **Interview Team** — a short set of questions a good coach would ask, and the one section here you fill in rather than generate. How you came across the role and what you already know about it, plus each person you expect to meet and what you have learned about them. Even one name sharpens what comes next: your answers feed Interview Prep below and give My Coach the context to help. These notes stay private to your account.
- **Interview Prep** — the questions this role's interview cycle is most likely to ask, each with a structured answer (STAR) drawn from your own experience. It reads whatever you put in Interview Team, so filling that in first makes this sharper.
- **About This Company** — a read on the company beyond what you would find on Glassdoor: news from the last 90 days, the employee voice, the leadership's public footprint, and watch-outs named honestly, with industry-specific signal (a hospital's CMS Star Rating, a pharma's FDA actions, a nonprofit's 990 filings, and similar) woven in where it bears on your decision — in the market read, or as a watch-out. Every numeric or named factual claim cites its source URL inline; the card uses live web search. The **View job description** link in the section rail keeps the posting you submitted retrievable at any time, read-only, in exactly the text Reimagine built the playbook from (lightly cleaned up for reading). Note that Reimagine keeps that text, not the original file — an uploaded PDF is read into text on submission and the file itself is not stored, so what comes back is the posting's words, not a formatted document.
- **Compensation Read** — a sourced pay range for this role and market, triangulated across public salary sites (Glassdoor, BuiltIn, Salary.com, PayScale, and similar), with every dollar figure a clickable link to its source so you can check it yourself. On an Opportunity it looks up the company's size from public sources (the employee band on its LinkedIn page, and similar) and opens the read by naming the basis it anchored to — the company's size, the industry, and the market — since company size moves senior-role pay a lot. It sets aside sources that are matching a mislabeled version of the role (for example, manager-level pay showing up under a chief-officer title) so a stray outlier doesn't distort the range. For a senior or C-suite officer role at a sizable or prominent organization, it does more than screen outliers: generic salary sites systematically under-price officers (they pool the title with far-smaller employers), so the read reaches instead for the sources that actually price officers — IRS Form 990 officer-compensation filings for a nonprofit and its peers, SEC proxy statements (DEF 14A) for a public company — and anchors on that peer set. Where only generic data exists, it reports it but labels it a floor for that seniority, not the market rate. If it got the basis wrong, a "Did we get this wrong?" box under the card lets you correct the size, industry, or market, and it rebuilds the estimate against what you tell it. The relevant public sources still disagree among themselves, so the card names the honest spread across them rather than one falsely precise number, and it uses live web search. It is a starting point for your own research, not a verdict — a disclaimer under the card says exactly that. Build it when you want it.
- **Offer & Negotiation** — built on your Compensation Read, and written the way a seasoned executive compensation advisor would: where the offer sits against the *right* benchmark (for a senior or officer role, the employer's or peers' actual officer pay from public filings when the About This Company read surfaced it, not just a generic range), what to ask for, how it lines up with the practical priorities you set in Orientation, and the full package quantified. It reads the offer holistically — in trade-offs, not in isolation — the way an advisor would talk you through it. It gives you the package in totality (the firm money you can bank on versus the at-risk upside), then surfaces every trade-off that will shape your decision — as many as the offer presents, not a fixed few — such as trading a one-time sign-on for base that compounds into every future raise; how much equity or bonus risk to carry given your stability-vs-upside lean; or whether the money offsets a term that cuts against a priority you named, like travel — each framed as what you'd gain, what you'd give up, and what the call hinges on, never a verdict. The asks that follow are a sequenced plan (lead with the strongest, and what to pivot to if it's held), each carrying the specific target and the exact sentence you can say — and the case is built *forward*: the argument for more is the value you'll create for **this** employer (money you'll make or save, risk you'll reduce), with a past win used only as proof you can deliver it, never as the whole argument. Because you know your intended impact better than we can infer it, a **Strengthen your case** box lets you add, in your own words, anything that backs your asks — the value you'll create here, unvested equity you're forfeiting to leave (grounds for a make-whole sign-on), a real cost the move imposes like a longer commute, or a competing offer — and on rebuild the analysis builds each ask on it. And because the puts and takes are a conversation, a **Talk through the trade-offs with My Coach** button hands you off to your coach — who holds the same offer, priorities, and reasoning — to work them one at a time and help you land where it's right for *you*. It also states your real first-year and steady-state total compensation in dollars, rather than pointing you at a checklist. The case is always evidence — the return your work produced, tied to the number, never "you deserve more" as an assertion. Every deal point is data-backed rather than a want (the *Making Your Own Weather* way): where a sourced figure exists it's cited with a link you can click, and for the components we don't have a number for — a sign-on, an equity term, PTO, severance — the analysis points you to the authoritative source you can pull it from (Levels.fyi and executive-comp surveys, the employer's 990 or proxy filing, SHRM or BLS benefits data), so you walk in with evidence for each point, not an ask. You can give it your offer two ways: type a quick number in the optional field, or **upload the actual offer letter** (PDF, Word, or paste the text) and Reimagine breaks it into its parts — base, bonus, equity, sign-on, benefits, PTO, start date, contingencies, and more — into an editable list you can correct. That list is collapsed by default behind a **What we found in your offer** twisty — it's mainly there to confirm we parsed the terms right, so it stays out of the way until you open it. Blank fields are terms the letter didn't mention, which are often the best things to ask about. If we misread a field, edit it in place; there's no need to start over. When the company comes back with a better letter after you negotiate, use **Upload an updated offer** to replace the terms — it re-runs your guidance on the new numbers and shows you **what changed since your previous offer** (base, sign-on, equity, and your first-year total, each with the difference), so the result of your negotiation is right in front of you. Reimagine reads only the compensation and employment terms — never Social Security numbers, bank details, or your home address — and it does not keep the document; only the parsed terms are saved to your account. The parsed offer feeds the guidance in place of a typed number. When you upload or paste a new letter, Reimagine refreshes the Offer & Negotiation guidance off the new terms on its own — you don't rebuild it by hand — so what you see always reflects the latest offer. (The Compensation Read isn't re-run; it's market data for the role and company, and your offer doesn't change it.) A **Price your benefits** panel lets you add a few optional numbers focused on what actually moves your take-home: your monthly health premium (what comes out of your check), the 401(k) match, any employer HSA contribution, and PTO days. When you upload an offer letter, the fields the letter states — the 401(k) match, employer HSA, deductible, and a plain PTO day count — are filled in from it, so you're mostly confirming rather than re-typing. The letter is the source of truth for those, so uploading an updated letter refreshes them to the new numbers, overwriting an earlier value. The one number the letter usually doesn't carry — your own monthly health premium — is never touched, and a PTO stated as "Unlimited" leaves your own estimate alone. Enter each the way the offer states it — the 401(k) match as a percent of base (say "3%") is fine, and Reimagine converts it to dollars against your base rather than reading it as three dollars. Once you've entered a number or two, press **Calculate** and the panel shows what these benefits are worth to you per year, net of what you pay for health, plus your first-year total. The comparison later weighs the money that comes back to you against what you pay for health — so an offer with a higher salary but a costlier plan doesn't look better than it is. It's entirely optional; anything you skip simply isn't counted. A **Model your bonus** control sits alongside it: set the attainment you actually expect (for a percent-of-base bonus) or a dollar figure (for a dollar or commission bonus) and press Calculate to see the payout at that level. A target is the optimistic case, so the bonus is kept separate from the firm number rather than baked into it — the honest figure to lean on is what you can bank on without the bonus. If the offer includes equity, a **How to read the equity** panel sets the company stage (public, late-stage private, early-stage private) and frames it honestly — a private grant sits behind investors and can be worth nothing, so it's deliberately held out of every total and left as a personal call to weigh with your Coach. The analysis also includes a **How it fits what matters to you** read — it measures the offer against the practical non-negotiables you set in the Your Priorities & Non-Negotiables step of Orientation (your compensation floor, commute or remote needs, deal-breakers, and stability-vs-upside lean) and flags conflicts (a base under your floor, a hybrid role when you need remote, a deal-breaker the offer appears to hit) as things to confirm before you decide. This part is a fit read, never a verdict — it never tells you whether to accept; that call is yours. (It only appears when you've set priorities.) Once the analysis is built, a **Prepare for the conversation** button condenses it into a printable set of **talking points** — what to confirm before you decide, what to ask for (each with the one line to say), and what to get in writing — that you can take into the conversation or paste into a follow-up. Once you've built them, they refresh on their own whenever the analysis is rebuilt — after you upload an updated offer letter, say — so they never fall out of sync with the deal. A **Print / Save as PDF** button opens it as a clean, one-page Reimagine document. The card also carries two things you can use straight away without generating anything: a **total compensation checklist** (bonus payout history, benefits detail, equity, PTO utilization, and more) and a short **negotiation script library**. For the offer situations that don't have a universal answer — severance timing, take-it-or-leave-it or algorithm-set offers, reading a company's layoff history — it points you to a real conversation with My Coach. It only names market figures your Compensation Read already sourced, so build that first. Whether to accept is always your call.
- **Who You Know Here** — which of your own LinkedIn connections already work at this company, so you can ask for an introduction rather than apply cold. It needs one thing from you, once: your LinkedIn connections file. LinkedIn lets you download your own connection list from its data download page (on a computer — the export does not work on phones); you pick **Connections**, request the archive, and LinkedIn emails you a link. That one can take up to two days to arrive, and the link works for 72 hours, so it is worth requesting before you need it. Drop the **Connections.csv** into the card and Reimagine checks it against the company on this opportunity, and against every opportunity you add afterwards, without being asked again. **Your file never leaves your computer.** It is read in your browser and nothing in it is sent to Reimagine, which also means it stays on the machine you loaded it on and My Coach cannot see it. Matching is on the employer each connection has listed, so a name shows as an exact match or as a close one with the company as they wrote it. It finds first-degree connections only: LinkedIn does not let any outside tool see who your connections are connected to, and says so plainly in its own documentation. For the people one step past that, the card carries a link that opens LinkedIn with the search already narrowed to this company and to second-degree connections. That page is worth more than it looks: under every name, LinkedIn lists the connections you and that person share. Reimagine cannot see those, and LinkedIn exports them to nobody — but on their own site they are right there, and they are the introduction path. Find someone senior enough to matter, look at who you both know, and ask that person to make the introduction. That search is deliberately wide rather than filtered to current employees, which means it also surfaces people who *used to* work there — often the most candid conversation you can get about why a seat is open and who really decides, since they are no longer protecting a relationship. (Those people are invisible to the matching above: LinkedIn's export tells Reimagine only where each connection works right now, never where they worked before.) The wide search also catches a company that trades under several names, which is the usual case rather than the exception — a single mining group turned up seven separate LinkedIn company pages, and the one where an actual search result worked was not even among them. LinkedIn's own "Current companies" filter would make you pick one of those and quietly drop the rest. The trade-off for staying wide is noise when the company's name is a common word. If that happens, the **Edit** control on the "Matching on" line lets you change the company this card works from — a parent, a subsidiary, or what they used to be called — and add a term to narrow the LinkedIn search, such as a function or a city. Both re-run as you type, at no cost, because nothing here is generated. Narrowing terms steer the LinkedIn search only; which of your own connections match is always decided by the employer each one has listed. Be aware too that LinkedIn limits how many search results a free account can view each month, so heavy searching can hit a wall that has nothing to do with Reimagine. A blank result can mean an out-of-date file as easily as no connection, so the card shows when you loaded it and offers a replacement when it has been a while. Next to each person, Reimagine will draft the message for you — in the same voice as your Cover Letter, but aimed at someone who already knows you. It asks first what you actually want from that person, because the reasons differ and so do the notes. Four choices: **Find out who's hiring** asks who owns the role or who would know, which is a small question with a useful answer and the one to send when your application needs to reach the right desk. **Learn about the place** asks for fifteen minutes on what it is really like there, with no request for help getting hired attached. **Ask them to put in a word** is the heaviest ask and the strongest move you have with someone who knows your work first-hand. **Just reconnect** makes no request at all — it gets you back in touch and mentions you are looking, which is the right note for someone you have not spoken to in years. Each keeps its own draft, so you can look at two angles on the same person before choosing.

There is also an optional line where you can say how you know them. It is worth filling in: without it the note stays on the present, because Reimagine will not invent a shared project, a mutual colleague, or a past conversation you did not describe. With it, the draft can open on the real thing and sounds like it came from you. One build gives you two versions behind a toggle: an **Email** and a shorter **LinkedIn message**, the way Resume Refresh gives you a Human and an ATS version of one resume. Send the email whenever you can; a LinkedIn message only arrives if that person happens to be checking LinkedIn. Most connections withhold their address in their LinkedIn privacy settings, so when yours is one of those, the card explains the conventions most employers use, takes the company's mail domain from you, and lays out the likely forms of their address to try — labelled as patterns to confirm, never as an address Reimagine knows — with a pointer to Hunter.io to check one. Drafting a note is the one moment anything about a connection is sent anywhere: to write it, Reimagine passes that person's first name, their job title, and how long you have been connected — the same things anyone can read on their LinkedIn profile. Your connections file itself is still never uploaded, and the finished drafts are kept on your computer alongside it rather than in your account.
- **Recruiters for This Role** — you found this specific opening, so you don't need a recruiter to reach it. Where they pay off is the *next* role like this one: the executive-search firms that specialize in this function and industry are worth knowing, because the specialist chasing this kind of seat is exactly who you want in your network. Reimagine reads the job description to work out the function, industry, and level, then finds the firms that specialize in that combination — boutique firms pointed at directly, and the named practice leaders at the big firms. A name shows only when it traces to a live first-party source (the firm's own page or the person's own LinkedIn); otherwise the card points you to the practice page rather than guess. When a firm has a relevant search open right now, a green flag surfaces it — an adjacent live role you can act on today. If the read of the posting is off or thin, an **Edit** control on the "Matching on" line lets you correct the function, industry, seniority, or geography and rebuild. You get one outreach note to adapt per firm, and the list downloads as a CSV or prints. Build it when you want it.

Each section is independent. About This Company, Compensation, and Where you fit are already underway from the moment you submit; for the others, build the ones that help most for the role you care about and leave the rest. Each rail entry shows a check once it is built, so you can see your progress at a glance. Click any entry to jump to it.

## How to find it

**Add an Opportunity** sits in the sidebar under **Put It to Work**, alongside Career Paths. It is also the second of the two cards on the Put It to Work screen itself.

It becomes available as soon as your Personal Brand exists, because the playbook reads your brand to judge fit. You do not have to explore a direction first, and you can come back and add another opportunity at any point.

## On the screen

The intro screen explains what the module is for. A small **What to bring** callout reminds you that Reimagine works best with the actual posting text (not a summary), and that adding your own context about the role makes the playbook sharper. If you know who told you about the role, what they said about the team, why you are interested, or anything about your relationship to the hiring manager, add it to the text field alongside the JD.

Below the intro:

- An option to upload a PDF of the job description
- A divider
- A textarea where you can paste the job description text instead

You can use either. If you upload a PDF, Reimagine extracts the text and shows it in the textarea so you can edit before generating. If you paste, the text goes straight into the textarea.

Each time you add an opportunity, the form starts empty, so you are always pasting a fresh posting rather than editing a prior one. Your existing opportunities stay saved in My Playbooks; adding a new one never replaces them.

When you have the JD in place, click **Build My Playbook**. Reimagine creates the opportunity record and lands you on the playbook within about a second, then immediately starts building your first cards for you, from the top down. About This Company comes first: it gathers industry context, recent moves, and how this company tends to evaluate candidates. Compensation follows with a sourced pay range for the role and market. Then Where you fit, a quick read on what the posting is asking for. A short line at the top tells you what is building and what is coming, and each card appears as it finishes. The remaining cards wait for you to choose what to build next. About This Company and Compensation run live web search, so they take a little longer; Where you fit lands in about half a minute once it starts. The saved playbook is titled from the posting itself, the company and the role, a moment after you submit, and you can rename it any time from My Playbooks.

## What good looks like

- **Where you fit should be honest, not flattering.** If the role is off-path from the direction you are pursuing, the section should say so plainly and explain why. Reimagine coaches; it does not gatekeep. The decision to pursue is yours.
- **The bridge story should sound like you saying it out loud.** It is the finishing beat of Where you fit: a draft you refine into a 30-second answer you can deliver in a screening call without sounding memorized.
- **The Resume Refresh should foreground the competencies this role asks for.** The repositioned summary and the strongest accomplishments shift; the rest of your resume can stay as it is.
- **Address the cover letter to the most relevant leader, by first name.** Reimagine reads your About This Company research and opens the letter to the executive whose area covers the role — first name only, no "Dear" and no title. When no clear person fits (relevance matters more than seniority, so it will not just grab the CEO), it addresses the team by function ("Hi Product Team,") rather than "Dear Hiring Manager." Build About This Company first so the letter has a name to work with.
- **Interview Prep should give you the actual questions this role's interview cycle will ask**, with the raw material from your own inputs to build each answer. The strongest version is in your voice, with the specifics only you can add.
- **You can practice any of those answers on the spot.** Under every question there is a **Practice this answer** button. It opens a box where you answer that question in your own words, with a microphone so you can say it out loud instead of typing. **Get My Coach's feedback** sends the question and your answer straight to My Coach and takes you there, with the feedback already on its way. My Coach reads what you actually said and tells you what is working and what to sharpen. When you have filled in Interview Team, the practice box also carries the name of the person who is likely to ask, so the feedback is tuned to that conversation. Nothing from the practice box is saved; the conversation lives in My Coach like any other.
- **About This Company should surface one or two findings you would not have thought to look for.** Industry-specific signal is woven in where it bears on your decision — a hospital's CMS Star Rating and Leapfrog Safety Grade, a pharma's FDA actions — surfacing in the market read, or as a named watch-out when it is a concern. Every numeric claim cites a source URL inline. Watch-outs are named plainly, not softened.

## How to refine

Each section has its own Refine box. Examples:

- "You missed that the role explicitly requires P&L experience. Emphasize that more."
- "My time at [Company] was internal strategy, not consulting. Recast Where you fit with that in mind."
- "The STAR stories are too leadership-heavy. The role is more individual contributor. Pick stories that show that."
- "The Resume Refresh skews too marketing-flavored. The role is more operations; rework the accomplishments to match."

Use the Refine box on the section you want to change. The corrections you submit persist on your profile and propagate to any later Focus Playbooks or Opportunity Playbooks you build for other roles, just like factual corrections elsewhere in the tool (Refining and Regenerating covers this in detail).

## Saved with each opportunity

Each opportunity is saved on its own, with whichever sections you have built. You can come back to it later and find it where you left it, and you can work several opportunities at once without one replacing another. In My Playbooks, your opportunities appear together under **Opportunity Playbooks**, listed separately from your direction-level **Focus Playbooks**. The section count on each card ("3 of 6 sections built") shows you at a glance how far along that opportunity is. It counts the six generated sections; Interview Team is something you fill in rather than build, so it does not add to the total.

When you have logged an offer on two or more opportunities, a **Compare offers** button appears in My Playbooks and lines them up side by side around the money you can actually bank on. My Playbooks covers how to read it.

Opportunities you saved before the card-based rebuild keep their original shape, exactly as you built them, with their own Refine box. Nothing you did is lost. New opportunities use the card shape described above. If you want the card shape for an older opportunity, paste its job description again through Add an Opportunity and build the sections you need.

## Notes

Every opportunity has a **Notes** card for anything worth remembering about it. Write what happened after a call, a detail someone mentioned in passing, a name you want to keep straight — whatever you would otherwise lose track of between conversations.

Three kinds of thing collect there:

- **Your note** — anything you type in yourself.
- **From My Coach** — a reply you chose to save onto this opportunity while chatting with My Coach.
- **Completed** — a next step you finished, filed automatically with the date so the record survives after you clear it.

Notes are yours alone. Nothing you write there feeds what Reimagine generates for you, so you can be as rough and unedited as you like — it will not turn up in your Resume Refresh or your Interview Prep. If you want something to shape what gets written, put it in the context box on Interview Team instead, which is read when your sections are built.

Remove any note with the Remove button on it.

## Save as PDF

Save as PDF builds a polished single-document version you can keep, share, or come back to. The persistent gold footer button appears as soon as one card is built, and an inline cue appears at the bottom once the generated sections are all done. The output is a clean, paged PDF with each section on its own page.
