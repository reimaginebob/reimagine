# My Playbooks

**My Playbooks is where your work lives.** Every role you explore and every opportunity you add is saved here, and this is the screen you come back to.

It is a permanent entry in the sidebar, available from anywhere in the app, from the moment you have a Personal Brand.

## What is on the screen

The page opens with **My Playbooks** and a line telling you how many you have saved out of the limit (ten). Below that, your work is split into two groups, because they are different kinds of thing and you navigate them differently.

**Focus Playbooks.** One per role you opened from Role Options. Each card carries the role title, a gold badge naming the direction it came from (Familiar Ground, Industry Insider, or Work That Matters), how many of its sections are built, and when you last touched it.

**Opportunity Playbooks.** One per job description you brought in through Add an Opportunity. Each card carries the company and role, an **Opportunity** badge, its section count, and when you last touched it.

Each group has an add button in its heading: **Explore More Roles** takes you back to the directions, and **Add an Opportunity** starts a new one from a posting. If a group is empty, a short note in its place tells you what would go there.

## What you can do with a card

- **Open.** Picks the playbook back up exactly where you left it. Your sections, your inputs, and your refinements are all there.
- **Rename.** Click the title and type. A pencil appears when you hover, and the change saves when you click away or press Enter. Useful when the auto-generated title is not how you think of the role.
- **Remove.** Takes it out of your set. Reimagine asks you to confirm first.

The section count on each card ("5 of 8 sections built") is the fastest way to see how far along something is when you come back after a week away.

## Compare offers

When you have logged an offer on two or more Opportunity Playbooks, a **Compare offers** button appears at the top of the page.

It lines the offers up side by side around a "cash and benefits you can bank on" line: base pay, plus the benefits that come back to you (401(k) match, HSA, PTO), minus what you pay for health. That is the firm money, so an offer with a higher salary but a costlier health plan does not look better than it is. Two offers a few thousand dollars apart in salary can flip once the premium is counted.

Bonus and equity sit on their own lines below, since what they are really worth depends on attainment and on an exit no one can predict. The bonus line shows the payout at the attainment level you modeled, with a separate "with bonus, if you hit it" line so you can see the difference without treating it as guaranteed. Equity is held at $0 in every total, with its stage and any paper value noted beside it, because a private grant can be worth nothing and counting it would flatter the offer.

Anything you did not price shows as "not counted." The comparison never ranks the offers or tells you which to take. It puts the full picture in front of you and leaves the decision where it belongs.

## Working across several playbooks at once

You can have several playbooks going at the same time, up to ten. They do not interfere with each other.

Your Personal Brand is constant across all of them. Only the per-role work (The Role, your Bridge Story, the market research, the resume and LinkedIn copy) gets written fresh for each one. So exploring a second direction never costs you the first.

If you build a playbook for a role you have already built, or bring in the same job description twice, Reimagine quietly re-links you to the existing playbook instead of creating a duplicate. Nothing is lost; you land back in the same record.

## Coming back to your work

Sign in from any device with the same email you used originally, and your work loads automatically. Nothing is stored on one machine and nothing needs to be exported and re-imported. Saving and Sharing Your Work covers the details of how account-based saving works.

Some people open My Playbooks daily during an active search; others weekly. The point is that the destination is stable: everything you generated is here, and every section is a couple of clicks away.

## When to open what

A short field guide for the sections inside a playbook:

- **Your Personal Brand.** Read before any conversation where someone is going to ask "what do you do." It is the spine of every other answer, and it sits outside the playbooks because it does not change with the role.
- **The Role.** Read before any conversation about whether this role is the right fit for you.
- **Your Bridge Story.** Read aloud before a phone screen or an interview. The goal is to put the three parts back in your head so they are familiar rather than memorized.
- **Industry Background.** Review before networking events, informational calls, and any first conversation in this space.
- **Interview Prep.** Review before any interview. The STAR stories inside it are worth reading before any conversation that might surface them, and worth remixing in real time based on who is across the table.
- **Resume Refresh.** Open your resume document and apply the changes, or download the Word file and work from that.
- **LinkedIn Remix.** Copy the headline and About section into LinkedIn. The output stays here for reference; the work happens on your profile.
- **Go-to-Market.** Your operating manual for the search itself. Who to reach, where, and the draft to start from. The Download CSV button turns the company list into a working outreach tracker.
- **Income Now.** Your fastest path to revenue while you search, if you generated it.

## Turn a playbook into a podcast about you

Here is one of the more interesting things people do with the PDF, and it is more useful than it sounds.

Save a Focus Playbook or an Opportunity Playbook as a PDF, upload it into **Notebook LM** (Google's free notebook tool at [notebooklm.google.com](https://notebooklm.google.com)), and use the **Audio Summary** feature. Notebook LM generates a short podcast-style conversation between two hosts who read the document, react to your background, and discuss it out loud.

It is a little bit fun. It is also surprisingly useful. There is something about hearing someone else talk about you that is validating in ways reading the same words on a page cannot match. When the hosts react to a specific accomplishment, or call out something that makes your combination distinctive, you get an unexpected outside perspective on the work you just built.

A few minutes to set up. A few minutes of audio to listen back. The effect tends to be grounding, often surprisingly so. It is the moment Reimagine's output stops being words on a page and starts feeling like something you can carry into a conversation.

## When to come back and refine

Reaching a full playbook does not close anything. If a conversation surfaces something new, a project you forgot to mention, a passion you should have flagged, a refined direction, you can go back to the relevant input in the sidebar, change it, and regenerate from there. Refining and Regenerating covers the mechanics.
