# Go Independent

> **Who this chapter is for.** Go Independent is a separate entry track for people who have already decided to work for themselves and are building a consulting or fractional-executive practice. You reach it through its own link, usually from a community or partner that sent you here. If you signed up through the normal career.club front door, this chapter does not describe what you are seeing, and nothing here is missing from your account.

Most of Reimagine is built around a question: what work should I go after, and how do I get it? Go Independent starts one step past that. You already know what you do. What you need is a way to say it that a stranger understands, a list of people who actually need it, and a plan for what to charge.

The engine underneath is the same one everyone else uses. Your Personal Brand is built from the same evidence, in the same way. What changes is the framing at the front door, one extra question during Orientation, and where you land once your brand is written.

## What is different

**Before we start.** A short screen between Welcome and the first question, laying out what Orientation is about to ask for, why each piece matters, and the three things it turns into. Nothing to fill in; it exists so the screens that follow read as a build rather than a form.

**Orientation is nearly identical.** Your resume, LinkedIn, assessment, values, priorities, reputation, story, and skills are all asked the same way, and they matter for the same reasons. Two things change.

The first is that you are not asked the questions that only make sense for someone with an employer. How your job search is going, and whether you want remote, hybrid, on-site, or relocation, are all shapes an employer offers. None of them appear. Your country and city stay, because the client research uses them.

The second is a screen the standard track does not have.

**Where You Think You Fit.** Two questions, placed right after Reputation so that your own read on yourself sits next to what other people say about you. The first asks what problem, gap, or need you think you are the answer to. The second asks what the company that has that problem looks like.

This is the most useful screen on the track, and it is worth slowing down for. A category gives Reimagine somewhere to start looking. A specific description gives it a stage, a sector, a trigger, and a doubt to speak to, and the client list it builds for you later is only ever as sharp as this answer.

Your answer here is a hypothesis, not a commitment. Reimagine tests it against your background and against live research, and where it sees things differently, it says so.

**Your Practice, In One Line.** After your Personal Brand is written, the standard track offers two doors: explore Career Paths, or add a specific opportunity. Neither fits someone who already knows what they do, so this track replaces both with one short screen.

It hands you a single sentence describing what you sell, drafted from your Personal Brand and from your own answers on Where You Think You Fit. You edit it until it sounds like you. If the draft is off in a way editing will not fix, Draft me another comes at your offer from a different angle.

The sentence is the thing to get right, because everything after it is built from it. Aim for something you would actually say out loud when someone asks what you do, not a tagline:

> Fractional VP of Marketing for Series B and C SaaS companies preparing to scale go-to-market.

You can change it later, from the same screen. Sections you have already built will show that they were written against an earlier version, and you can refresh whichever ones you want.

**No Career Paths, no Deep Dive.** This track does not generate a menu of practice concepts for you to browse and pick from. You are not deciding what to do; you are sharpening how you say the one thing you already do.

## Your practice plan

Confirming your one line takes you to your practice plan. It holds the same generations the Focus Playbook does, ordered the way the work actually runs and named for what they do here.

**1. Find Your Clients.** Live research into specific companies that fit who you help and why, plus how to reach the right person at each one. It starts from the buyer you named in Where You Think You Fit and treats that as a starting point to confirm, sharpen, or argue with, rather than a brief to follow. Where the research lands somewhere you did not expect, it tells you why and shows you what took it there.

**2. Price, Pitch & Launch.** This is Income Now, rebuilt for a practice. On the standard track, Income Now is money coming in while a search runs. Here, the practice is the goal, so the plan covers what to charge, how to package the work, where your buyers already gather, and the immediate next steps to take. Nothing in it is framed as a stopgap.

**Everything else you'll need**, built as you want them and in any order:

- **Your Pitch.** The 30-second version of who you are and why this work, anchored on your earned authority to operate on your own. Told to someone deciding whether to buy, not to someone deciding whether to hire.
- **Your LinkedIn.** Headline, About, and how to reframe your experience now that you work for yourself.
- **Your One-Sheet.** Your background rewritten as a credentials document rather than a job application.
- **Discovery Call & Pitch Prep.** The questions a prospect asks, worked through, with the same Practice this answer box the standard track has.

Three sections from the standard playbook do not appear here, because none of them has a job to do for someone selling their own services: The Role reads a job description against a candidate, Industry Background teaches you the language of a sector you are entering, and Recruiters for This Path finds people who place candidates into roles.

## What is the same

Everything not listed above. Your Personal Brand, My Coach, My Playbooks, saving and printing, refining a section that misses, and the way corrections carry across sections all work exactly as the rest of this guide describes. Your practice saves to My Playbooks like any other playbook and can be downloaded as a PDF or as plain text.

## A note on what is still coming

The deeper business-of-consulting material — managing a pipeline, renegotiating rates with an existing client, holding scope boundaries, planning capacity — is not built yet. It is deliberately waiting for real questions from real practices rather than guessed ones. If you have those questions now, ask My Coach; it will answer from what it knows about you, and what people actually ask is what gets built next.
