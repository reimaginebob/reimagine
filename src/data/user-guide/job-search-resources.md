# Job Search Resources

Job Search Resources is in the sidebar from your first day, before you have built anything. It is there from the start on purpose: the help it points to costs nothing and does not wait for you to be further along.

It answers one question. Where can you go, near you, to do this alongside other people.

## Career Club Corner

The screen opens with Career Club Corner, which is Bob Goodwin's free weekly call. It runs every Monday at 12:00 ET, it is free, and every session is recorded, so a Monday you cannot make is not a Monday you miss. It teaches the five-step process from *Making Your Own Weather*, which is the same method Reimagine is built on.

Two things happen there that are hard to get on your own. The first is the people: a network of others who understand the search you are in and who are connected to people you have never met. The second is that it is a place to practise. You can try your Tell Me About Yourself out loud, get it wrong, hear about it, and fix it before it costs you anything.

## Groups near you

Below that, Reimagine looks for what is running where you live. It searches for the kinds of help that are free or close to it:

- **Public library job-seeker programs.** Many libraries run a weekly job-search group with coaching, classes, and employers who hire out of it.
- **All-volunteer job-search groups.** Often decades old, often meeting weekly, and almost never advertised.
- **Your American Job Center.** The publicly funded workforce system. Free workshops, one-to-one help, and job-search planning, everywhere in the country.
- **Faith-based and community career ministries.** Many run open groups you do not have to be a member to attend.
- **College and community-college career services**, a number of which are open to people who are not students.
- **Professional bodies that run a job-transition group**, which several do alongside their regular programming.

Every result shows what it costs before you click anything. If a group charges dues, the card says so.

Add your city on Your Current Situation and this search can look near you. Without it, the rest of the screen still works.

## Why we name groups and not dates

Reimagine will tell you that a group meets weekly, or on the third Tuesday. It will not tell you the date of the next meeting.

That is deliberate. Meeting dates are the part of this that goes out of date fastest, and old event pages stay findable long after the meeting happened and sometimes long after the group itself has folded. Rather than hand you a date that might send you to an empty room, each result links the group's own page, which is the version that stays current.

For the same reason, when a local chapter has merged into a larger one, Reimagine points you at the organisation that still exists rather than the page that still ranks.

## The live searches

At the bottom are four links that are not saved lists. They run a fresh search when you open them, so they show whatever is happening now: job-search groups near you, the same search on LinkedIn, career and business groups on Meetup, and the American Job Center finder.

Meetup is strongest for technology, product and startup work and thinner in other fields, so treat an empty result there as a fact about Meetup rather than about your field.

## Two things worth asking about

Neither of these shows up in any search.

If your last employer provided outplacement, many outplacement firms run open group sessions, and you may still be entitled to them.

And when you meet someone new, ask whether they are in a group they have found useful. A recommendation from someone who has been through it is better than anything a search can hand you.

## Choosing a group

Groups vary, and two things tell you quickly whether one is worth your time.

A good group spends most of its time on the work: what people are trying, what landed, what each person is doing this week. If the main activity is agreeing about how bad the market is, that is a different kind of meeting, and it will cost you time you need.

The second is the difference between a group and a feed. Trading encouraging comments online feels like community and takes about as long as the real thing, without the introductions or the accountability that make a group worth joining.
