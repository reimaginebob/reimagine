# 11b. Add an Opportunity

> *(Bridge Story structure updated 2026-05-19; see [07-tell-your-story.md](07-tell-your-story.md) for the current model.)*

This is the **Add an Opportunity** card on the Put It to Work screen in Phase 2 (Chapter 6). Whenever you have a specific job description in hand, choose Add an Opportunity, paste it in, and Reimagine creates an Opportunity Playbook for that role with five sections. Two of them start building the moment you submit the posting; the rest you build on demand. It is the most natural reason to come back to Reimagine during an active search: every specific role you consider can get its own playbook here. You no longer have to finish the main journey first; Add an Opportunity is available as soon as you reach the Put It to Work screen, and the sidebar entry under Phase 2 routes back there from anywhere in the app.

## When to use it

The full Reimagine journey gives you a strategy. Who you are, where you fit, three directions through your possible options, a chosen target, a bridge story, a target company list, refreshed LinkedIn and resume, and a Focus Playbook.

That strategy gets you ready to act. Specific roles come into view: a posting your network forwarded you, a job description you found on LinkedIn, an opportunity you uncovered through your outreach. When one of those lands in your sights and you want to be sharp for it, this module gives you five sections you can build for that exact role.

Use it when:

- You are seriously considering applying to a specific posting
- You have a screening call or first conversation scheduled
- You want to know whether a role is the right fit before you invest more time in it
- You want a Bridge Story or interview prep tuned to this specific posting

## What this module produces

You paste a job description or upload it as a PDF. Reimagine creates an Opportunity Playbook scoped to that role with seven sections. Three of them — About This Company, Compensation, and The Role — start building the moment you submit the posting, because they need nothing from you beyond the JD itself. The rest you build when you want them. Every section is saved with this opportunity:

- **The Role** — an honest read on alignment with the direction you are pursuing, where the role stretches you, and the watch-outs you should know going in. It finishes with a **30-second bridge story** tuned to this posting — the case above in words you can say out loud. The bridge builds automatically right after the read, and you can refine it in its own feedback box until it sounds like you saying it.
- **Resume Refresh** — a repositioned summary and key accomplishments that emphasize the competencies this role asks for.
- **Cover Letter** — a short draft letter tuned to this posting, in your own voice. It is the sibling of your Go-to-Market outreach: the same direct, human energy, adapted for when you are responding to a posting. It is part of your application packet with your bridge story (built into The Role) and Resume Refresh, tuned so they read as one voice rather than repeating each other. It draws on those two, so building them first makes the letter stronger (it will still generate without them). It addresses the company by name, so it needs a posting Reimagine can identify the company from.
- **Interview Prep** — the questions this role's interview cycle is most likely to ask, each with a structured answer (STAR) drawn from your own experience.
- **About This Company** — a read on the company beyond what you would find on Glassdoor: news from the last 90 days, the employee voice, industry-specific signal (CMS Star Ratings for hospital systems, GitHub cadence for tech, FINRA filings for financial services, and similar), the leadership's public footprint, and watch-outs named honestly. Every numeric or named factual claim cites its source URL inline; the card uses live web search. A **View job description** button sits just above this card: the posting you submitted stays retrievable at any time, read-only, in exactly the text Reimagine built the playbook from (lightly cleaned up for reading). Note that Reimagine keeps that text, not the original file — an uploaded PDF is read into text on submission and the file itself is not stored, so what comes back is the posting's words, not a formatted document.
- **Compensation Read** — a sourced pay range for this role and market, triangulated across public salary sites (Glassdoor, BuiltIn, Salary.com, PayScale, and similar), with every dollar figure a clickable link to its source so you can check it yourself. On an Opportunity it looks up the company's size from public sources (the employee band on its LinkedIn page, and similar) and opens the read by naming the basis it anchored to — the company's size, the industry, and the market — since company size moves senior-role pay a lot. It sets aside sources that are matching a mislabeled version of the role (for example, manager-level pay showing up under a chief-officer title) so a stray outlier doesn't distort the range. For a senior or C-suite officer role at a sizable or prominent organization, it does more than screen outliers: generic salary sites systematically under-price officers (they pool the title with far-smaller employers), so the read reaches instead for the sources that actually price officers — IRS Form 990 officer-compensation filings for a nonprofit and its peers, SEC proxy statements (DEF 14A) for a public company — and anchors on that peer set. Where only generic data exists, it reports it but labels it a floor for that seniority, not the market rate. If it got the basis wrong, a "Did we get this wrong?" box under the card lets you correct the size, industry, or market, and it rebuilds the estimate against what you tell it. The relevant public sources still disagree among themselves, so the card names the honest spread across them rather than one falsely precise number, and it uses live web search. It is a starting point for your own research, not a verdict — a disclaimer under the card says exactly that. Build it when you want it.
- **Offer & Negotiation** — built on your Compensation Read, and written the way a seasoned executive compensation advisor would: where the offer sits against the *right* benchmark (for a senior or officer role, the employer's or peers' actual officer pay from public filings when the About This Company read surfaced it, not just a generic range), what to ask for, how it lines up with the practical priorities you set in Orientation, and the full package quantified. It reads the offer holistically — in trade-offs, not in isolation — the way an advisor would talk you through it. It gives you the package in totality (the firm money you can bank on versus the at-risk upside), then surfaces every trade-off that will shape your decision — as many as the offer presents, not a fixed few — such as trading a one-time sign-on for base that compounds into every future raise; how much equity or bonus risk to carry given your stability-vs-upside lean; or whether the money offsets a term that cuts against a priority you named, like travel — each framed as what you'd gain, what you'd give up, and what the call hinges on, never a verdict. The asks that follow are a sequenced plan (lead with the strongest, and what to pivot to if it's held), each carrying the specific target and the exact sentence you can say — and the case is built *forward*: the argument for more is the value you'll create for **this** employer (money you'll make or save, risk you'll reduce), with a past win used only as proof you can deliver it, never as the whole argument. Because you know your intended impact better than we can infer it, a **Strengthen your case** box lets you add, in your own words, anything that backs your asks — the value you'll create here, unvested equity you're forfeiting to leave (grounds for a make-whole sign-on), a real cost the move imposes like a longer commute, or a competing offer — and on rebuild the analysis builds each ask on it. And because the puts and takes are a conversation, a **Talk through the trade-offs with My Coach** button hands you off to your coach — who holds the same offer, priorities, and reasoning — to work them one at a time and help you land where it's right for *you*. It also states your real first-year and steady-state total compensation in dollars, rather than pointing you at a checklist. The case is always evidence — the return your work produced, tied to the number, never "you deserve more" as an assertion. Every deal point is data-backed rather than a want (the *Making Your Own Weather* way): where a sourced figure exists it's cited with a link you can click, and for the components we don't have a number for — a sign-on, an equity term, PTO, severance — the analysis points you to the authoritative source you can pull it from (Levels.fyi and executive-comp surveys, the employer's 990 or proxy filing, SHRM or BLS benefits data), so you walk in with evidence for each point, not an ask. You can give it your offer two ways: type a quick number in the optional field, or **upload the actual offer letter** (PDF, Word, or paste the text) and Reimagine breaks it into its parts — base, bonus, equity, sign-on, benefits, PTO, start date, contingencies, and more — into an editable list you can correct. That list is collapsed by default behind a **What we found in your offer** twisty — it's mainly there to confirm we parsed the terms right, so it stays out of the way until you open it. Blank fields are terms the letter didn't mention, which are often the best things to ask about. If we misread a field, edit it in place; there's no need to start over. When the company comes back with a better letter after you negotiate, use **Upload an updated offer** to replace the terms — it re-runs your guidance on the new numbers and shows you **what changed since your previous offer** (base, sign-on, equity, and your first-year total, each with the difference), so the result of your negotiation is right in front of you. Reimagine reads only the compensation and employment terms — never Social Security numbers, bank details, or your home address — and it does not keep the document; only the parsed terms are saved to your account. The parsed offer feeds the guidance in place of a typed number. When you upload or paste a new letter, Reimagine refreshes the Offer & Negotiation guidance off the new terms on its own — you don't rebuild it by hand — so what you see always reflects the latest offer. (The Compensation Read isn't re-run; it's market data for the role and company, and your offer doesn't change it.) A **Price your benefits** panel lets you add a few optional numbers focused on what actually moves your take-home: your monthly health premium (what comes out of your check), the 401(k) match, any employer HSA contribution, and PTO days. When you upload an offer letter, the fields the letter states — the 401(k) match, employer HSA, deductible, and a plain PTO day count — are filled in from it, so you're mostly confirming rather than re-typing. The letter is the source of truth for those, so uploading an updated letter refreshes them to the new numbers, overwriting an earlier value. The one number the letter usually doesn't carry — your own monthly health premium — is never touched, and a PTO stated as "Unlimited" leaves your own estimate alone. Enter each the way the offer states it — the 401(k) match as a percent of base (say "3%") is fine, and Reimagine converts it to dollars against your base rather than reading it as three dollars. Once you've entered a number or two, press **Calculate** and the panel shows what these benefits are worth to you per year, net of what you pay for health, plus your first-year total. The comparison later weighs the money that comes back to you against what you pay for health — so an offer with a higher salary but a costlier plan doesn't look better than it is. It's entirely optional; anything you skip simply isn't counted. A **Model your bonus** control sits alongside it: set the attainment you actually expect (for a percent-of-base bonus) or a dollar figure (for a dollar or commission bonus) and press Calculate to see the payout at that level. A target is the optimistic case, so the bonus is kept separate from the firm number rather than baked into it — the honest figure to lean on is what you can bank on without the bonus. If the offer includes equity, a **How to read the equity** panel sets the company stage (public, late-stage private, early-stage private) and frames it honestly — a private grant sits behind investors and can be worth nothing, so it's deliberately held out of every total and left as a personal call to weigh with your Coach. The analysis also includes a **How it fits what matters to you** read — it measures the offer against the practical non-negotiables you set in the Practical Priorities step of Orientation (your compensation floor, commute or remote needs, deal-breakers, and stability-vs-upside lean) and flags conflicts (a base under your floor, a hybrid role when you need remote, a deal-breaker the offer appears to hit) as things to confirm before you decide. This part is a fit read, never a verdict — it never tells you whether to accept; that call is yours. (It only appears when you've set priorities.) Once the analysis is built, a **Prepare for the conversation** button condenses it into a printable set of **talking points** — what to confirm before you decide, what to ask for (each with the one line to say), and what to get in writing — that you can take into the conversation or paste into a follow-up. Once you've built them, they refresh on their own whenever the analysis is rebuilt — after you upload an updated offer letter, say — so they never fall out of sync with the deal. A **Print / Save as PDF** button opens it as a clean, one-page Reimagine document. The card also carries two things you can use straight away without generating anything: a **total compensation checklist** (bonus payout history, benefits detail, equity, PTO utilization, and more) and a short **negotiation script library**. For the offer situations that don't have a universal answer — severance timing, take-it-or-leave-it or algorithm-set offers, reading a company's layoff history — it points you to a real conversation with My Coach. It only names market figures your Compensation Read already sourced, so build that first. Whether to accept is always your call.

Each section is independent. About This Company, Compensation, and The Role are already underway from the moment you submit; for the others, build the ones that help most for the role you care about and leave the rest. A section rail on the left of the page lists the sections; each entry shows a check once it is built, so you can see your progress at a glance. Click any entry to jump to it.

## How to find it

After you complete the main journey, **Add an Opportunity** appears in the sidebar below Income Now. The Complete screen also includes a callout pointing here.

Until you finish Complete, the sidebar entry is dimmed and not clickable. The module is built on everything you produce in the main flow, so it cannot be used until that foundation is in place.

## On the screen

The intro screen explains what the module is for. A small **What to bring** callout reminds you that Reimagine works best with the actual posting text (not a summary), and that adding your own context about the role makes the playbook sharper. If you know who told you about the role, what they said about the team, why you are interested, or anything about your relationship to the hiring manager, add it to the text field alongside the JD.

Below the intro:

- An option to upload a PDF of the job description
- A divider
- A textarea where you can paste the job description text instead

You can use either. If you upload a PDF, Reimagine extracts the text and shows it in the textarea so you can edit before generating. If you paste, the text goes straight into the textarea.

Each time you add an opportunity, the form starts empty, so you are always pasting a fresh posting rather than editing a prior one. Your existing opportunities stay saved in My Playbooks; adding a new one never replaces them.

When you have the JD in place, click **Build My Playbook**. Reimagine creates the opportunity record and lands you on the playbook within about a second, then immediately starts building your first cards for you, from the top down. About This Company comes first: it gathers industry context, recent moves, and how this company tends to evaluate candidates. Compensation follows with a sourced pay range for the role and market. Then The Role, a quick read on what the posting is asking for. A short line at the top tells you what is building and what is coming, and each card appears as it finishes. The other cards (Resume Refresh, Cover Letter, and Interview Prep) wait for you to choose what to build next. About This Company and Compensation run live web search, so they take a little longer; The Role lands in about half a minute once it starts. The saved playbook is titled from the posting itself — the company and the role — a moment after you submit, and you can rename it any time from My Playbooks.

## What good looks like

- **The Role should be honest, not flattering.** If the role is off-path from what you chose in Phase 2, the section should say so plainly and explain why. Reimagine coaches; it does not gatekeep. The decision to pursue is yours.
- **The bridge story should sound like you saying it out loud.** It is the finishing beat of The Role: a draft you refine into a 30-second answer you can deliver in a screening call without sounding memorized.
- **The Resume Refresh should foreground the competencies this role asks for.** The repositioned summary and the strongest accomplishments shift; the rest of your resume can stay as it is.
- **Interview Prep should give you the actual questions this role's interview cycle will ask**, with the raw material from your own inputs to build each answer. The strongest version is in your voice, with the specifics only you can add.
- **About This Company should surface one or two findings you would not have thought to look for.** The industry-specific scoreboard is the load-bearing subsection: for a hospital system, the CMS Star Rating and the Leapfrog Safety Grade carry decision-weight; for a tech company, the engineering blog cadence and GitHub activity do. Every numeric claim cites a source URL inline. Watch-outs are named plainly, not softened.

## How to refine

Each section has its own Refine box. Examples:

- "You missed that the role explicitly requires P&L experience. Emphasize that more."
- "My time at [Company] was internal strategy, not consulting. Recast The Role with that in mind."
- "The STAR stories are too leadership-heavy. The role is more individual contributor. Pick stories that show that."
- "The Resume Refresh skews too marketing-flavored. The role is more operations; rework the accomplishments to match."

Use the Refine box on the section you want to change. The corrections you submit persist on your profile and propagate to any later Focus Playbooks or Opportunity Playbooks you build for other roles, just like factual corrections elsewhere in the tool (Chapter 12 covers this in detail).

## Saved with each opportunity

Each opportunity is saved on its own, with whichever sections you have built. You can come back to it later and find it where you left it, and you can work several opportunities at once without one replacing another. In My Playbooks, your opportunities appear together under **Opportunity Playbooks**, listed separately from your direction-level **Focus Playbooks**. The section count on each card ("2 of 4 sections built") shows you at a glance how far along that opportunity is.

When you have logged an offer on two or more opportunities, a **Compare offers** button appears in My Playbooks. It lines the offers up side by side around a "cash and benefits you can bank on" line — base pay, plus the benefits that come back to you (401(k) match, HSA, PTO), minus what you pay for health. That's the firm money, so an offer with a higher salary but a costlier health plan doesn't look better than it is; two offers a few thousand dollars apart in salary can flip once the premium is counted. Bonus and equity sit on their own lines below, since what they're really worth depends on attainment and on an exit no one can predict — the bonus line shows the payout at the attainment level you modeled, with a separate "with bonus, if you hit it" line so you can see the difference without treating it as guaranteed. Equity is held at $0 in every total, with its stage and any paper value noted beside it — a private grant can be worth nothing, so counting it would flatter the offer. Anything you didn't price shows as "not counted," and the comparison never ranks the offers or tells you which to take; it just puts the full picture in front of you.

Opportunities you saved before this update keep their original shape: the full ten-section playbook, exactly as you built it, with its own Refine box. Nothing you did is lost. New opportunities you start from here use the five-section shape described above. If you want the five-section shape for an older opportunity, paste its job description again through Add an Opportunity and build the sections you need.

## Save as PDF

When the playbook is complete, Save as PDF builds a polished single-document version you can keep, share, or come back to. The persistent gold footer button appears as soon as one card is built, and an inline cue appears at the bottom of the cards once all five are done. The output is a clean, paged PDF with each section on its own page.
