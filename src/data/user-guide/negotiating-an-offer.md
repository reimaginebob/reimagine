# Negotiating an Offer

Most of what you need to negotiate well is stable enough to write down once. The Offer & Negotiation section in your Opportunity Playbook carries it: a sourced pay range for the role, a total compensation checklist so you ask about the whole package and not just base, and a few scripts that do a lot of work in the conversation. Build the Compensation Read, enter your offer if you have one, and you have the number, the range it sits against, and the language to open the conversation.

But some of the hardest questions in a negotiation don't have a universal answer. They depend on the company, the moment, and your own read of the situation. Those belong in a conversation with My Coach, which knows your profile and can reason about your specific case. This chapter covers the ones that come up most, so you know what to bring to that conversation.

## The number, framed as evidence, not entitlement

The strongest ask rests on evidence. Rather than claiming you deserve more, you show what you did, the return it produced, and the number that reflects it. Your Offer & Negotiation section already builds this for you — it pulls a concrete accomplishment from the work you did in Bridge Story and Interview Prep and ties it to the number. Confidence in a negotiation comes from evidence you can point to, not from asserting your worth. When you walk in with the proof, you don't have to claim the value; the value is already on the table.

## Negotiating severance before you accept

This one depends on the specifics. Asking about severance terms before you've accepted an offer is uncommon, and how a company reacts to the question tends to track its size and maturity — a larger, more established employer is more likely to have a framework for it and to take the question in stride than a small company where the concept may not exist. It is not a reason to hold back if the security matters to you, but go in knowing it is a less-traveled ask, and let the size and formality of the employer shape how you raise it. My Coach can help you think through whether and how to bring it up for your specific situation.

## Reading a company's stability before you push

How hard you push is partly a read on the company itself. Recent layoffs, a shaky funding position, or a market under pressure change the leverage on both sides. The goal is to calibrate the ask to what you can see, sizing the push to the moment rather than walking away from it. Public signal helps here: the About This Company section gathers recent news, and you can bring what you find to My Coach to think through what it means for your negotiation.

## Offers with no room to negotiate

Some offers are set by an algorithm or a rigid band with no discretion at the table, and you'll sometimes be told directly that the number is the number. When that's real, the useful move is a clear read on the labor market and your own leverage — a better script won't move a fixed number. Look at how in-demand the skill set is, how replaceable the role is, and whether there's a non-cash lever (title, start date, remote terms, a review timeline) even when base is fixed. My Coach can help you find the lever that actually exists in a situation like this rather than pushing on one that doesn't.

## Who you're actually talking to

Who you negotiate with shapes what's possible. A recruiter or HR contact is often relaying a range they don't own; the hiring manager is usually the person who actually wants you and has more room to advocate internally. It's worth knowing which conversation you're in, and when it makes sense to move the compensation conversation toward the person with the most stake in bringing you on. This is a judgment call about your specific situation — a good one to talk through with My Coach.

## The through-line

Static content owns the stable half of negotiating: the checklist, the scripts, the sourced number. This chapter and My Coach own the half that's a judgment call. Reimagine won't tell you which offer to take or hand you a single right answer to a situational question — the decision is always yours. What it does is put the full picture on the table and give you a place to reason through the parts that don't fit a script.
